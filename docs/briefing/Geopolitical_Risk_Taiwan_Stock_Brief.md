## I. Overview

## II. Caldara and Iacoviello (2022) – "Measuring Geopolitical Risk"

### A. Main Themes
- **Development of a GPR Index:**  
  Introduces a novel news-based index to quantify geopolitical risk.
- **Macroeconomic Impact:**  
  Higher GPR is linked with declines in investment, employment, and stock prices, as well as a higher probability of economic disasters, lower expected GDP growth, and increased downside risks.
- **Threat vs. Realization:**  
  Distinguishes between the "threat" and the "realization" of adverse events, both negatively impacting economic activity.
- **Firm-Level Effects:**  
  Industries and firms more exposed to geopolitical risk experience larger declines in investment.

### B. Key Ideas and Facts
- **Definition of GPR:**  
  _"We define geopolitical risk as the threat, realization, and escalation of adverse events associated with wars, terrorism, and any tensions among states and political actors that affect the peaceful course of international relations."_
  
- **Construction of the Index:**  
  The index is built by counting articles that mention adverse geopolitical events in leading newspapers from the US, UK, and Canada.
  
- **Historical Spikes:**  
  Notable increases in the index coincide with events like the World Wars, the Korean War, the Cuban Missile Crisis, and 9/11.
  
- **Components of the Index:**  
  Consists of two parts: **geopolitical threats (GPT)** and **geopolitical acts (GPA)**.
  
- **Macroeconomic Effects (US):**  
  A GPR shock induces persistent declines in investment, employment, and stock prices, driven by both threat and realized events.
  
- **Cross-Country Analysis:**  
  Higher GPR values are associated with a higher probability of economic disasters, lower expected GDP growth, and increased downside risks.
  
- **Firm-Level Analysis:**  
  Industries more exposed to geopolitical risks see a larger decline in investment; idiosyncratic geopolitical risk is linked with reduced firm-level investment.
  
- **Index Validation:**  
  Validated through comparisons with a narrative index based on the New York Times front pages and by aligning with related data (e.g., military spending, economic policy uncertainty).
  
- **Impact Summary:**  
  Increased geopolitical risk leads to a short-lived rise in financial uncertainty.

### C. Methodology
- Construction of a news-based index using a specific dictionary related to geopolitical events.
- Utilization of VAR models to analyze macroeconomic effects of GPR shocks.
- Incorporation of cross-country data to examine links between GPR and economic disasters.
- Firm-level investigation to study the impact of GPR on investment.

### D. Limitations and Caveats
- The index may not capture slowly unfolding geopolitical events that are identified only with hindsight.
- The focus on historical events might not fully represent future geopolitical risks.

## III. "GRP_Taiwn_stock.pdf" – Geopolitical Risk and Taiwan Stock Prices

### A. Main Themes
- **Taiwan-Specific GPR Index:**  
  Construction of a daily GPR index for Taiwan using data from local news sources.
- **Impact on Taiwanese Stocks:**  
  Analysis of how GPR affects the equity prices of Taiwanese companies, especially those investing in China.
- **Event Studies:**  
  Evaluation of the effects of the Russian invasion of Ukraine and Nancy Pelosi's visit to Taiwan.
- **Local and Foreign Risks:**  
  Emphasizes the need to consider both local and international geopolitical risks when analyzing financial markets.

### B. Key Ideas and Facts
- **Motivation:**  
  Investigates geopolitical risks in Taiwan given its strategic geopolitical position and its complex relations with China and the United States.
  
- **Methodology:**  
  Adapts Caldara and Iacoviello's methodology to develop a daily GPR index tailored for Taiwan.
  
- **Event Study Outcomes:**  
  - Firms with investments in China were adversely affected by the Russian invasion.
  - The Pelosi visit did not show a significant impact.
  
- **Combined GPR Analysis:**  
  A combined analysis using Taiwan, China, and overall GPR indices indicates that increases in GPR significantly affect the stock returns of firms with investments in China.
  
- **Data and Variables:**
  - Daily stock closing prices of Taiwanese listed firms (January 1, 2021 – December 31, 2023)
  - Taiwan Stock Exchange Capitalization Weighted Stock Index (TAIEX)
  - Firm-specific details, including investments in China.

### C. Limitations and Caveats
- Measurement of China investment is limited by its reliance on a binary indicator and investment ratio, possibly overlooking finer details of exposure.
