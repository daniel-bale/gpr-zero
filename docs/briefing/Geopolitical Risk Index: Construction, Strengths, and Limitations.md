The Geopolitical Risk (GPR) index is constructed through a process involving definition, measurement, and validation.

**Definition:** Geopolitical risk is defined as the threat, realization, and escalation of adverse events related to wars, terrorism, and tensions among states and political actors that disrupt peaceful international relations. This definition includes not only violent conflicts and territorial disputes but also power struggles and terrorism. The index captures a range of adverse geopolitical events, from threats to realization and escalation, aligning with media practices that use "geopolitical risks" as a catch-all phrase. The index is broken down into separate "acts" and "threats" components.

**Measurement:** The GPR index is constructed using automated text searches of news articles. The methodology involves:
*   **Data Collection:** The text from approximately 25 million news articles published in leading English-language newspapers from 1900 to the present are analyzed. The recent GPR index (from 1985) uses data from ten newspapers: Chicago Tribune, Daily Telegraph, Financial Times, Globe and Mail, the Guardian, Los Angeles Times, New York Times, USA Today, the Wall Street Journal, and the Washington Post. The historical GPR index (from 1900) uses the historical archives of the Chicago Tribune, the New York Times, and the Washington Post.
*   **Dictionary-Based Method:** A dictionary of words associated with geopolitical events and threats is used. The search query specifies two words or phrases whose joint occurrence likely indicates adverse geopolitical events. The dictionary includes "topic words" (e.g., "war," "nuclear," "terrorism") and "threat" or "act" words. The search query is organized around eight categories.
*   **Search Query:** The search query is organized in eight categories. Each category is captured by a search query comprising two sets of words, the first set containing topic words (e.g., “war,” “nuclear,” or “terrorism”), the second set containing “threat” words for categories 1 through 5 and “act” words for categories 6 through 8.
*   **Calculating the Index:** The GPR index counts the share of articles discussing adverse geopolitical events and associated threats each month.

**Strengths of the GPR Index:**
*   **Captures Key Events:** The GPR index effectively captures major geopolitical events over the past 120 years, as perceived by contemporaries. The index is highly correlated with a narrative index constructed by manually scoring the front pages of the New York Times from 1900 through 2019.
*   **Flexibility:** The GPR index can be sliced into separate  country-specific components, reflecting the different geographic imprint of major geopolitical events.
*   **Complements Existing Measures:** The GPR index captures information about geopolitical events that is not reflected in other indicators, such as military spending news, indicators of the human cost of conflicts, economic policy uncertainty, and financial volatility.
*   **High Reliability:** The Cronbach alpha, measuring internal consistency across indices based on the ten individual newspapers, is 0.96, indicating an excellent degree of reliability.

**Limitations of the GPR Index:**
*   **Media Dependence:** The index relies on media coverage, which may be influenced by factors other than the actual level of geopolitical risk.
*   **Potential for Misclassification:**  Doing so exclusively with  one-word searches would likely lead to misclassification and measurement error.
*   **Geographic Focus:** The index is constructed using US newspapers, which may reflect a US perspective on geopolitical risks.
*   **Exclusion of slowly unfolding events:** The index may not appropriately measure episodes that slowly unfold over multiple years and are recognized as geopolitical risks only with the benefit of hindsight.
*   **Changes in media attention**: Newspapers appear to have devoted increasingly more space to arts, history, sports, and entertainment, often borrowing some of their language from warfare and military terminology.
